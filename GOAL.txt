The required theorem must match the mathematical scope of Freek Wiedijk's currently credited HOL Light theorem:
https://www.cs.ru.nl/~freek/100/hol.html#43
https://github.com/jrh13/hol-light/blob/master/100/isoperimetric.ml

For every continuous, rectifiable, simple closed planar curve, let L be its geometric length and A the Lebesgue area of its bounded Jordan interior. Prove

    4 * Real.pi * A <= L ^ 2

and prove that equality implies that the image of the curve is a circle. Prove the converse for circles too, so that the final result characterizes equality.

Cover nonconvex curves, either orientation, and arbitrary continuous rectifiable parametrizations. Smoothness, convexity, constant speed, a halfspace description, an assumed area formula, and externally supplied Fourier identities are not permitted hypotheses of the final theorem. Establish any parametrization and analytic properties needed by the proof.

A concrete acceptable formulation uses:
- gamma : Real -> EuclideanSpace Real (Fin 2);
- ContinuousOn gamma (Set.Icc 0 1);
- gamma 0 = gamma 1;
- Set.InjOn gamma (Set.Ico 0 1);
- BoundedVariationOn gamma (Set.Icc 0 1).

Here Gamma is gamma '' Set.Icc 0 1, length is (eVariationOn gamma (Set.Icc 0 1)).toReal, and area is the real value of the actual Lebesgue volume of the bounded Jordan interior. Equivalent standard formulations using paths or interval subtypes are acceptable, provided that their equivalence to these geometric hypotheses is proved. Define the interior using connected components of the complement, or reuse MovingSofa's genuine Jordan interior; do not define area by a boundary integral or by the desired inequality.

The equality conclusion should identify Gamma with Metric.sphere c r for some planar c and r > 0. Prove that the area and length are finite, that the interior is measurable and bounded, and that the length is positive under the stated simplicity hypotheses. Do not rely on toReal's behavior on infinite measures. Do not demand literal-ball rigidity for arbitrary measurable sets: null-set modifications make that a different and potentially false statement. The required rigidity theorem concerns Jordan curves.

Suggested proof organization

First fix the final geometric statement and definitions in a small module whose dependencies and meanings can be independently inspected. Keep this statement stable while developing the proof.

A. Obtain the full curve inequality from Humanfia plus MovingSofa.
- Establish the Jordan interior's frontier is Gamma.
- Establish Hausdorff boundary length equals geometric curve length.
- Specialize n = 2, prove the unit-disk volume normalization, and transfer the inequality to A and L.

B. Develop usable arclength parametrization.
- Reparametrize every permitted curve as a Lipschitz loop on [0,1] with speed L almost everywhere.
- Prove preservation of the image, length, interior, and signed area, with orientation handled explicitly.
- Do not assume that an arbitrary rectifiable parametrization is absolutely continuous.

C. Prove the analytic rigidity argument at the regularity actually available.
- Extend periodic integration by parts and the sharp Wirtinger estimate to absolutely continuous loops with square-integrable derivative, or to an equivalent rigorously proved integral representation.
- Obtain a boundary integral formula for the actual area of the arclength-parametrized Jordan curve.
- Deduce that equality in the geometric bound forces a circular parametrization.

One promising route avoids a separate classification of equality in Brunn-Minkowski or Sobolev. For a positively oriented loop on [0,1], subtract its mean c, write z = gamma - c, let J be rotation by ninety degrees, and set

    E = integral ||z'||^2,
    X = integral ||z||^2,
    A = (1/2) * integral det(z,z').

Wirtinger gives E - (2*pi)^2 * X >= 0. Completing the square gives

    E - 4*pi*A
      = (1/2) * integral ||z' - 2*pi*J(z)||^2
        + (1/2) * (E - (2*pi)^2 * X).

For the arclength parametrization E = L^2. Equality therefore forces z' = 2*pi*J(z) almost everywhere. Solve this equation using absolute continuity to show the image is a circle, and handle reversed orientation. All identities and implications in this outline must be proved in Lean. An alternative complete proof is acceptable if it reduces the work; the final geometric scope remains fixed.

D. Assemble the final theorem for the original arbitrary parametrization.
- Prove the area-length inequality and equality iff the image is a circle.
- Supply the positive-radius circle case and verify sharpness using actual geometric area and length.
- Expose a concise public theorem with precisely the geometric hypotheses stated above.

Smooth or convex cases may be useful internal milestones, but they do not complete this goal. General n-dimensional rigidity or a complete finite-perimeter library is not required.
